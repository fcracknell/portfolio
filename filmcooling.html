Interferometric Characterisation of Liquid-Film Entrainment in Rocket Engines

Overview

Liquid-film cooling is a common technique for protecting rocket combustion chamber walls from extreme heat fluxes, whereby a thin liquid film is injected along the wall and progressively thins as it entrains into the hot core flow downstream. Because these films are only tens to hundreds of microns thick and highly sensitive to disturbance, accurately measuring their thickness and entrainment behaviour with conventional intrusive probes is difficult.

As part of the Monash Summer Research Scholarship, I developed and validated a non-intrusive, interferometry-based measurement technique for characterising these liquid-film cooling layers. A Python-based optical ray-tracing simulation was built to model how light interacts with the thin liquid film, enabling interference patterns to be related back to local film thickness with high spatial resolution.
